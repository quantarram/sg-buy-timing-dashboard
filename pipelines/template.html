The supply cycle

A COE lasts ten years, so cars registered ten years ago are a schedule of expiries. De-registrations track that schedule closely and set the quota, and the premium has followed the supply cycle with a lag of about 18 to 24 months (a correlation of about −0.7). The schedule now stands at about {{SCHED_NOW}} vehicles a month, {{SCHED_1Y}} a year ahead, against {{DEREG_NOW}} actual de-registrations over the last 12 months, so quota is set to keep rising through 2027 to 2028.

That would normally ease premium pressure, but the premium reached record highs through 2024 to 2026 while de-registrations were already rising, so demand or a longer lag is overriding it for now. This is a description of where the market sits in the cycle, not a forecast of a turning point: there are only two full cycles in the data.

What the evidence supports

  • Mostly a base-rate problem. Across {{ENTRIES}} out-of-sample entries the average lift of the admitted conditions is {{OVERALL_LIFT}}: modest, and uneven from year to year.
  • One supported mechanism. After a quota cut the premium was in its top 20% of moves 15% of the time one month later (13% base), 24% after three months (16%), 34% after six (17%) and 50% after twelve (22%). Quota increases were not followed by lower premiums.
  • Nothing else beat the base rate reliably: interest rates, short-run de-registrations and momentum added little.
  • Limits. Monthly data from 2002, a rule change in quota in 2022 and 2023, two supply cycles, public data only, no costs of ownership.

HDB resale: look up a block

Every registered HDB resale since March 2012 (HDB, via data.gov.sg). Type a block number and street, or a town. Prices depend on the unit's storey, size, renovation and remaining lease, so a block's recent sales are a guide to its range, not a valuation.
