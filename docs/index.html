The supply cycle

A COE lasts ten years, so cars registered ten years ago are a schedule of expiries. De-registrations track that schedule closely and set the quota, and the premium has followed the supply cycle with a lag of about 18 to 24 months (a correlation of about −0.7). The schedule now stands at about 6,770 vehicles a month, 7,573 a year ahead, against 5,181 actual de-registrations over the last 12 months, so quota is set to keep rising through 2027 to 2028.

That would normally ease premium pressure, but the premium reached record highs through 2024 to 2026 while de-registrations were already rising, so demand or a longer lag is overriding it for now. This is a description of where the market sits in the cycle, not a forecast of a turning point: there are only two full cycles in the data.

What the evidence supports

  • Mostly a base-rate problem. Across 3,292 out-of-sample entries the average lift of the admitted conditions is 1.31: modest, and uneven from year to year.
  • One supported mechanism. After a quota cut the premium was in its top 20% of moves 15% of the time one month later (13% base), 24% after three months (16%), 34% after six (17%) and 50% after twelve (22%). Quota increases were not followed by lower premiums.
  • Nothing else beat the base rate reliably: interest rates, short-run de-registrations and momentum added little.
  • Limits. Monthly data from 2002, a rule change in quota in 2022 and 2023, two supply cycles, public data only, no costs of ownership.

HDB resale: look up a block

Every registered HDB resale since March 2012 (HDB, via data.gov.sg). Type a block number and street, or a town. Prices depend on the unit's storey, size, renovation and remaining lease, so a block's recent sales are a guide to its range, not a valuation.

Worked example of the method on one block: timing the sale of a five-room flat at BLK 184 Jelebu Rd.

What the property evidence supports

  • Nothing beats the base rate in the latest decade. Across 1,622 admitted condition-years the out-of-sample lift is 0.96 overall and 0.64 since 2017 (1.0 is no better than the base rate).
  • Momentum worked in one cycle. Price momentum scored a lift of 3.6 up to 2016 (largely the 2008 to 2010 swings) and 0.7 since 2017. The same fade shows in: 2-year government yield change, rent growth.
  • Why. Only about six real price cycles in the data, and policy measures from 2011 on may have changed how the market behaves; neither is tested here.
  • So the page shows risk, not a call: the full drawdown history and the base rates over both windows.

Replay of the base-rate forecasts over the past

The forecast for each anchor is the share of the last ten years’ windows (and, separately, of all earlier windows) in which the series was higher after the horizon. A Brier score of 0.250 is what a coin flip scores; lower is better. The ten-year base rate beat the coin in 2 of 18 series and horizons, the all-history base rate in 10. For COE the mean score is 0.261 (ten-year) and 0.254 (all history): about a coin flip. For the property series it is 0.285 and 0.234: the long history is the better guide, because a trailing decade over-reacts to the regime just before it.
